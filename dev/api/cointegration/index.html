API: Cointegration and VECM

The estimated cointegrating relation between log GNP and log consumption in econ5, with its autocorrelation — the display that shows whether an "equilibrium" actually behaves like one, and one no Python library draws. The worked explanation is in Manual: Cointegration and VECM.

using TSAnalytics, Plots
e = dataset("econ5")
Y = (gnp = log.(e.gnp), consum = log.(e.consum))
plot(cointegration_plot(fit_vecm(Y; lags = 2, rank = 1)); size = (750, 300))

Residual-based tests

TSAnalytics.coint_test — Function
coint_test(Y; trend=:const, lags=nothing, autolag=:aic) -> CointTest

The Engle-Granger (1987) two-step test of the null of no cointegration: regress the first column of Y on the others (and the deterministic terms), then run an augmented Dickey-Fuller test with no deterministic terms on that regression's residual. A residual with no unit root means some linear combination of the series is stationary.

The first column is the dependent variable

The statistic is not symmetric in the ordering of the columns — swapping them gives a different number, and can give a different conclusion. Put the variable you would normalize the cointegrating relation on first.

trend sets the deterministic terms of the cointegrating regression: :none, :const (default), :trend (constant and linear trend) or :quadratic (constant, trend and trend²) — statsmodels' "n", "c", "ct", "ctt". (Unlike fit_var's deterministic, :trend here includes the constant; a trend without one is not a case MacKinnon tabulates.)

Lags: pass them explicitly to reproduce a reference

lags is the ADF regression's lag order — a maximum when autolag is :aic, :bic or :tstat, the exact order when autolag = nothing. The default is adf_test's own Schwert rule, ⌈12 (n/100)^{1/4}⌉, and the selection is adf_test's, so the package has one lag-selection implementation rather than two.

[V] At a fixed lag the statistic is unambiguous: statsmodels' coint and an Engle-Granger built by hand from R's lm and urca::ur.df agree to 1.8e-15. With each selecting its own lag they land on different orders on three of four fixtures — coint3_r1 gives −9.57 in R and −12.27 in Python. The test is not what they disagree about; the search is. An autolag-selected statistic will therefore not in general match either reference, and an explicit lags with autolag = nothing reproduces both.

Tables, and what is refused

p-values are MacKinnon's (1994) response surfaces, which cover N ≤ 6 series; critical values are MacKinnon (2010) at sample size n − 1, covering N ≤ 12 — both transcribed from statsmodels (BSD) and checked against urca's independent 1995-vintage tables, which agree to about 5e-4 on critical values and 2e-2 on p-values.

  • N between 7 and 12: the critical values are returned and pvalue throws, naming them.
  • N > 12: refused — nothing is tabulated.
  • trend = :none: refused. MacKinnon's no-constant critical values are for a single series only. [V] statsmodels returns a p-value with NaN critical values beside it here; no field of this result is ever NaN.
  • A single series: refused, naming adf_test. [V] statsmodels silently turns that into a plain ADF test and reports N = 1 critical values, which is not a cointegration test.
julia> using TSAnalytics, DelimitedFiles

julia> Y = readdlm(joinpath(dirname(pathof(TSAnalytics)), "..", "test",
                            "fixtures", "var", "coint2_yes.csv"), ','; skipstart=1);

julia> t = coint_test(Y; lags=4, autolag=nothing);

julia> round(t.statistic; digits=6), t.lags
(-5.278984, 4)

julia> t.critical_values.levels
(1.0, 5.0, 10.0)

julia> pvalue(t) < 0.001
true

See also adf_test, critical_values.

TSAnalytics.CointTest — Type
CointTest <: HypothesisTest

The Engle-Granger cointegration test returned by coint_test.

  • statistic: the ADF t statistic on the cointegrating residual.
  • pvalue: the MacKinnon response-surface p-value, or nothing when the tables do not cover the case — then pvalue(t) throws, naming critical_values. pvalue_available says which.
  • critical_values: (levels = (1.0, 5.0, 10.0), values = (…)).
  • N (number of series), trend, lags (the lag actually used), autolag, nobs (series length).
  • beta: the cointegrating regression's coefficients, deterministic terms first — [const, x₂, …] for trend = :const; resid: the residual the ADF regression ran on.

TSAnalytics.phillips_ouliaris_test — Function
phillips_ouliaris_test(Y; kind=:Pz, deterministic=:const, bandwidth=:short)
    -> PhillipsOuliarisTest

The Phillips & Ouliaris (1990) residual-based tests of the null of no cointegration among the columns of Y (2 to 6 series):

  • kind = :Pu — the variance ratio (their (12)): T ω̂₁₁.₂ / (T⁻¹ Σ û²), the long-run conditional variance of the first series given the others against the residual variance of the cointegrating regression. Like coint_test it depends on which series is first.
  • kind = :Pz — the multivariate trace statistic (their (14)): T tr(Ω̂ M_zz⁻¹), invariant to the normalization — no series is singled out. That is why it is the default.

Both are right-tailed: a large value rejects. Ω̂ is the Bartlett- weighted long-run covariance of the residuals of a first-order VAR in the levels, with bandwidth lags: :short is ⌊4 (T/100)^{1/4}⌋ and :long ⌊12 (T/100)^{1/4}⌋, with T = n − 1 (5 and 15 at n = 250) — urca's rules — or an integer; 0 gives the uncorrected statistic.

deterministic is not a detail

:none, :const or :trend (constant and trend) — urca's demean = "none" | "constant" | "trend", the paper's "standard", "demeaned" and "demeaned and detrended". The choice changes the answer qualitatively: [V] on coint2_trend.csv, whose cointegrating relation contains a trend, Pu is 3.19 with :none (no rejection at 10%), 50.24 with :const (rejects at 5%) and 170.0 with :trend (rejects at 1%). The default is :const; choose deliberately.

Pz with a deterministic term follows the paper, not urca

With a constant or trend, Phillips & Ouliaris build M_zz from the demeaned (or detrended) data (§3, remark (f)), which is what their Tables IVb and IVc were simulated under. [V] urca::ca.po uses the raw z'z there. The consequence is not cosmetic: urca's statistic is not invariant to adding a constant to the data (71.86 becomes 38.84 on coint2_yes.csv after adding 1000 to both series), and in 4000 simulations of independent random walks at n = 500 it rejected 0.25% of the time at the nominal 5% with a constant and 0% with a trend, against 4.3% and 4.2% for the paper's statistic. Pu, and Pz with :none, agree with urca to 1e-10.

No p-value

The paper tabulates quantiles only, at seven sizes from 15% to 1%; critical_values reports the 10%, 5% and 1% points and reject compares against them. pvalue(t) throws. [V] tseries::po.test reports 0.01 or 0.15 — the bounds of its table — as though they were p-values; nothing here is censored that way.

m > 6 is refused: the tables stop at five regressors.

julia> using TSAnalytics, DelimitedFiles

julia> Y = readdlm(joinpath(dirname(pathof(TSAnalytics)), "..", "test",
                            "fixtures", "var", "coint2_yes.csv"), ','; skipstart=1);

julia> t = phillips_ouliaris_test(Y; kind=:Pu, deterministic=:const);

julia> round(t.statistic; digits=6), t.bandwidth
(143.609807, 5)

julia> t.critical_values
(levels = (10.0, 5.0, 1.0), values = (27.8536, 33.713, 48.0021))

julia> reject(t; level=1.0)
true

See also coint_test, reject.

TSAnalytics.PhillipsOuliarisTest — Type
PhillipsOuliarisTest <: HypothesisTest

A Phillips-Ouliaris residual-based cointegration test, returned by phillips_ouliaris_test. kind is :Pu or :Pz; m the number of series; bandwidth the Bartlett lag used for the long-run covariance. critical_values is (levels = (10.0, 5.0, 1.0), values) — ascending, since this is a right-tail test, the opposite of the MacKinnon tables' order; use reject rather than indexing by position. There is no p-value: pvalue(t) throws.

TSAnalytics.reject — Function
reject(t; level=5.0) -> Bool

Whether a test reported with critical values rejects its null at level percent, comparing in the test's own direction — left tail for coint_test (a statistic below the critical value rejects), right tail for phillips_ouliaris_test (above). level must be one of the levels in critical_values(t); it is matched by value, never by position, because the two families order their levels oppositely.

julia> using TSAnalytics, DelimitedFiles

julia> Y = readdlm(joinpath(dirname(pathof(TSAnalytics)), "..", "test",
                            "fixtures", "var", "coint2_no.csv"), ','; skipstart=1);

julia> reject(phillips_ouliaris_test(Y); level=10.0)   # independent random walks
false

TSAnalytics.critical_values — Function
critical_values(t::HypothesisTest) -> Union{Nothing, NamedTuple}

The critical values a test reports, as a NamedTuple whose levels field names the significance levels (in percent) explicitly — different tests order them differently (MacKinnon's tables run 1%, 5%, 10%; Phillips-Ouliaris and Johansen run 10%, 5%, 1%), so position is never load-bearing. nothing for a test that reports none.

Johansen

TSAnalytics.johansen_test — Function
johansen_test(Y; lags, deterministic=:const) -> JohansenTest

Johansen's likelihood-ratio tests for the cointegration rank of the K series in Y: the trace statistic −T Σ_{i>r} log(1 − λᵢ) for H₀: rank ≤ r against rank ≤ K, and the maximum-eigenvalue statistic −T log(1 − λ_{r+1}) against rank = r + 1, for r = 0 … K−1, with λ the eigenvalues of the reduced-rank regression (see _johansen_rrr). [V] With the mappings below, urca::ca.jo and statsmodels' coint_johansen agree with each other and with this to ≤ 2.2e-11.

lags is required, and counts differenced lags — statsmodels' k_ar_diff. urca's K is lags + 1 (it counts the levels VAR's order). lags = 0 is refused: [V] statsmodels silently accepts it.

deterministic: five cases, one shared between the references

deterministicmeaningurca ecdetstatsmodels det_orderOsterwald-Lenum
:noneno deterministic term—-1Table 0
:constunrestricted constant (a drift in the data)"none"0Table 1
:const_restrictedconstant only inside the cointegrating relation"const"—Table 1*
:trendunrestricted constant and trend——Table 2
:trend_restrictedunrestricted constant, trend inside the relation"trend"—Table 2*

:const is the common default and is urca's ecdet = "none" despite the name: "none" there means no deterministic term inside the cointegrating relation, and an unrestricted constant is still fitted. A restricted term adds a row to β and an eigenvalue that is zero by construction; it is dropped from the test sequence. statsmodels' det_order = 1 detrends the data rather than fitting this package's :trend model, so the two are not compared.

Critical values: Osterwald-Lenum (1992), and which table

Each case uses the Osterwald-Lenum table its own decision table assigns (p. 466). Two consequences worth knowing:

  • :const uses Table 1, so it agrees with statsmodels' values, not urca's. urca's ecdet = "none" values (5% trace for p − r = 1, 2, 3: 8.18, 17.95, 31.52) are Osterwald-Lenum's Table 1.1*, the case where the model has an unrestricted constant but the data's constant lies in the cointegrating space only — a table the paper itself calls superfluous under Johansen's (1992) testing procedure. Table 1's 3.76, 15.41, 29.68 is the matching case, and statsmodels' independently simulated 3.84, 15.49, 29.80 agrees with it.
  • The smallest-block value near 3.84 under :const and :trend is the asymptotic theory, not a defect: the last block is χ²(1)-like in exactly those cases.

The paper tabulates p − r ≤ 11, so K ≤ 11; more is refused, never answered with NaN — [V] which statsmodels does silently for 13 series and for det_order = 2.

No p-value

pvalue(t) throws, naming critical_values and coint_rank. MacKinnon-Haug-Michelis (1999) response surfaces would give real p-values — something neither reference has — but they are a separate, paper-sourced task, and interpolating three tabulated quantiles into a pseudo-p-value is not done.

urca's spec = "transitory" reparameterizes the short-run part only; the eigenvalues and statistics are identical ([V] to 5e-12), so it is not a separate option here.

julia> using TSAnalytics, DelimitedFiles

julia> Y = readdlm(joinpath(dirname(pathof(TSAnalytics)), "..", "test",
                            "fixtures", "var", "coint3_r1.csv"), ','; skipstart=1);

julia> t = johansen_test(Y; lags=1);

julia> round.(t.trace; digits=4)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 90.8401
  4.4815
  0.4595

julia> t.trace_crit[:, 2]          # 5% points, r = 0 first
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 29.68
 15.41
  3.76

julia> coint_rank(t)
1

See also coint_rank, normalize_beta, coint_test.

TSAnalytics.JohansenTest — Type
JohansenTest <: HypothesisTest

The Johansen cointegration rank tests returned by johansen_test. Everything is ordered r = 0 first, with the rank of each row in ranks so position is never load-bearing.

  • trace, maxeig: the statistics for H₀: rank ≤ r against rank ≤ K and against rank = r + 1.
  • trace_crit, maxeig_crit: K × 3, columns the 10%, 5% and 1% points of Osterwald-Lenum (1992) for the matching deterministic case.
  • eigenvalues: K of them, real and in [0, 1) by construction.
  • beta: the eigenvectors, K₁ × K with K₁ = K + 1 when a deterministic term is restricted to the cointegrating space (its coefficient is the last row); normalized β'S₁₁β = I, sign fixed so the first element is positive. Use normalize_beta for a conventional form.
  • alpha: the matching adjustment coefficients S₀₁β, so that alpha[:, 1:r] * beta[:, 1:r]' is the rank-r impact matrix.
  • lags counts differenced lags (statsmodels' k_ar_diff; urca's K is lags + 1); nobs is the effective sample.
source
TSAnalytics.coint_rank — Function
coint_rank(t::JohansenTest; statistic=:trace, level=5.0) -> Int

The cointegration rank chosen by Johansen's sequential procedure: test r = 0, 1, … in turn and stop at the first r the statistic does not reject at level percent (10, 5 or 1). If every hypothesis is rejected the rank is K — the levels are stationary.

julia> using TSAnalytics, DelimitedFiles

julia> Y = readdlm(joinpath(dirname(pathof(TSAnalytics)), "..", "test",
                            "fixtures", "var", "coint3_r2.csv"), ','; skipstart=1);

julia> coint_rank(johansen_test(Y; lags=1))          # one common trend, three series
2

TSAnalytics.normalize_beta — Function
normalize_beta(t, r; scheme=:first_unit) -> Matrix{Float64}

The first r cointegrating vectors in a conventional normalization:

  • :first_unit — each column divided by its first element, so the first row is all ones. This is urca::ca.jo's V.
  • :identity — the leading r × r block made the identity, β_r (β_r[1:r, :])⁻¹. This is urca::cajorls's β, and what most applied work reports for r > 1.

The two coincide at r = 1. The test statistics do not depend on the normalization; β does — [V] the references differ on it (statsmodels' evec is not normalized at all, and cajorls switches scheme with the rank), which is not a disagreement about the estimate. Beyond the first relation the vectors are only identified up to a rotation, so an over-specified r still returns numbers; they mean nothing more than the rank you asserted.

source
normalize_beta(m::VECMModel, r=m.rank; scheme=:first_unit) -> Matrix{Float64}

The fitted cointegrating vectors in a conventional normalization; see the JohansenTest method, which shares the implementation, so a Johansen test and a VECM with the same deterministic give the same normalized β.

A VECM stores β with an identity block (statsmodels' and cajorls' form). At rank 1 both schemes coincide. At rank 2 or more the :first_unit scheme is a property of the eigenvector basis that johansen_test returns, not of the cointegrating space, and the identity-normalized columns can lead with zero; it is refused here rather than divided by zero — use :identity, which agrees with the Johansen test at every rank.

VECM

TSAnalytics.fit_vecm — Function
fit_vecm(Y; lags, rank, deterministic=:const, method=:ml) -> VECMModel

Fit a vector error-correction model of cointegration rank rank with lags differenced lags (statsmodels' k_ar_diff; urca's K is lags + 1).

deterministic is johansen_test's five-value vocabulary, with the same meanings, so a rank chosen there and a model fitted here cannot silently refer to different specifications:

deterministicstatsmodelsurca
:none"n"—
:const"co" (constant outside the relation)ecdet = "none" + cajorls
:const_restricted"ci" (constant inside)ecdet = "const"
:trend"colo"—
:trend_restricted"coli"ecdet = "trend"

[V] "co" and "ci" are close — on coint2_yes, β₂ is −0.6673249 against −0.6673217 and the log-likelihoods differ by 0.022 — and they are different models.

Two estimators

  • method = :ml (default): Johansen's reduced-rank maximum likelihood — β from the same eigenproblem as johansen_test, then α, Γ and the deterministic terms by least squares given β. This is what all three references compute by default and what the Johansen rank test assumes. [V] urca::cajorls, statsmodels' VECM and tsDyn::VECM(estim = "ML") agree on β to ≤ 1e-14.
  • method = :twostep: Engle & Granger's two-step estimator — β from a static least-squares regression of the first series on the others (with any restricted deterministic term), then the error-correction regression. Consistent but not efficient, and a genuinely different estimate ([V] β = (1, −0.4931, −0.9655) against ML's (1, −0.4965, −0.9777) on coint3_r1); it matches tsDyn::VECM(estim = "2OLS"). Rank 1 only — [V] tsDyn warns and silently switches to ML when asked for more; this refuses instead.

Refusals

rank must be in 1 … K−1: rank 0 is a VAR in differences and rank K a stationary VAR in levels, and neither is a VECM. [V] statsmodels accepts both (returning an empty β for 0 and the identity for K), and also accepts k_ar_diff = 0, which is refused here too.

julia> using TSAnalytics, DelimitedFiles

julia> Y = readdlm(joinpath(dirname(pathof(TSAnalytics)), "..", "test",
                            "fixtures", "var", "coint3_r1.csv"), ','; skipstart=1);

julia> m = fit_vecm(Y; lags=1, rank=1);

julia> round.(vec(m.beta); digits=6)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
  1.0
 -0.496505
 -0.97773

julia> round.(vec(m.alpha); digits=6)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
  0.036929
 -0.234278
  0.960762

See also johansen_test, var_rep, forecast.

TSAnalytics.VECMModel — Type
VECMModel <: TimeSeriesModel

A fitted vector error-correction model, returned by fit_vecm:

ΔY_t = α β' Ỹ_{t-1} + Γ_1 ΔY_{t-1} + … + Γ_k ΔY_{t-k} + (unrestricted deterministic) + u_t

with Ỹ_{t-1} the lagged levels, augmented by a deterministic term when one is restricted to the cointegrating space.

  • alpha (K × r), beta (K₁ × r, K₁ = K + 1 when a term is restricted; its coefficient is the last row). beta is stored with its leading r × r block the identity — statsmodels' and cajorls' normalization — and alpha matches it, so alpha * beta' is the impact matrix. normalize_beta gives other forms.
  • gamma: lags matrices, each K × K; det_coef: K × n for the unrestricted deterministic terms (constant, then trend).
  • sigma_df (divisor T − q, q regressors per equation) and sigma_mle (divisor T).
  • stderr_alpha, stderr_gamma (K × K·lags), stderr_det: from the regression given β, with the ML Σ, as statsmodels computes them. stderr_beta: Lütkepohl's (2005, Prop. 7.6) asymptotic standard errors of the free elements of β, zero on the normalized block; nothing for method = :twostep, whose static regression gives none that are valid.
  • resid (T × K), loglik, nobs (T), lags (differenced lags), rank, deterministic, method.
source
TSAnalytics.var_rep — Function
var_rep(m::VECMModel) -> Vector{Matrix{Float64}}

The VECM written as a VAR in levels, lags + 1 coefficient matrices:

A₁ = I + α β' + Γ₁,    A_j = Γ_j − Γ_{j−1}  (j = 2 … lags),    A_{lags+1} = −Γ_lags

(β' restricted to its first K rows; a restricted deterministic term moves into the VAR's intercept or trend). This is what forecast and irf use — the VAR recursions of Stages 10.1 and 10.3, not a second implementation — and the test suite checks that the levels VAR reproduces the VECM's own residuals exactly.

[V] Matches vars::vec2var and statsmodels' var_rep.

The levels VAR has K − r unit roots by construction — that is what cointegration means — so it is not stable, and its long-run effects do not exist: lr_effects refuses a VECMModel.
